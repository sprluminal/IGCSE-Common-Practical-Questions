Recurring questions and markscheme answers

## Biology

| # | Typical question | Markscheme answer / points to include |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | How could the volume of oxygen or another gas be measured more accurately than by counting bubbles? | Collect the gas in a gas syringe and read its volume; an inverted measuring cylinder over water is an accepted alternative. |
| 2 | How could a vitamin C concentration or other endpoint measurement be made more precise? | Measure the reagent volume rather than counting drops; use intermediate concentrations, repeat the measurement, and/or use a larger sample or a more dilute titrant as appropriate. |
| 3 | What improvement would reduce error from adding an unmeasured volume of solution? | Use a syringe, graduated pipette, measuring cylinder with suitable graduations, or burette to measure the volume directly. |
| 4 | How could temperature be kept constant during an investigation? | Use a thermostatically controlled water bath. Insulation, a lid, or checking and reheating/top-up water are accepted in suitable setups. |
| 5 | Why can a changing water-bath temperature cause error, and how can it be reduced? | It means samples may be tested at different temperatures; maintain the bath at the set temperature and check it with a thermometer. |
| 6 | How could a subjective colour-change endpoint be made more accurate? | Compare against a colour chart or use a colorimeter; a white or black background can make the change easier to see. |
| 7 | How could contamination between solutions be prevented? | Use separate apparatus for each solution, or wash/rinse the syringe, pipette, glass rod, or measuring cylinder between uses. |
| 8 | Why is using one syringe for different solutions a source of error? | Residue contaminates the next solution and changes its concentration; use a clean or separate syringe for each solution. |
| 9 | How can differences in the biological samples be controlled? | Use the same type of tissue and make samples the same size, mass, surface area, or starting condition, as relevant. |
| 10 | How could an osmosis investigation using cylinders of tissue be improved? | Measure mass rather than length; use equal starting samples, soak for the same time, and dry them consistently before weighing. |
| 11 | How can the optimum concentration, pH, or temperature be found more accurately? | Test a narrower range around the apparent optimum, using smaller intervals between values. |
| 12 | How can a result between two tested concentrations be pinpointed? | Add intermediate concentrations (or other intermediate values of the independent variable) around the apparent threshold/optimum. |
| 13 | What should be done with repeated measurements? | Repeat each condition and calculate a mean; identify anomalous results where appropriate. Repeats improve reliability and reveal inconsistency. |
| 14 | How can an investigation establish more precisely which light intensity gives the greatest growth? | Measure growth at a narrower range of light intensities around the apparent maximum. |
| 15 | How can uneven mixing or inconsistent stirring be reduced as an error? | Stir or shake each sample in the same way and for the same duration; keep the timing/intervals consistent. |
| 16 | How could an error caused by unequal volumes be reduced? | Measure each volume with suitable graduated apparatus instead of estimating it; use the same measured volume for every trial. |
| 17 | How can a test of enzyme activity be made fair and more accurate? | Keep pH and temperature controlled, use the same volumes/concentrations, and time each reaction consistently. |
| 18 | How can a photosynthesis investigation avoid uncertainty from bubble size? | Measure gas volume over a fixed time (for example with a gas syringe), or measure the time to produce a fixed gas volume. |
| 19 | How can an investigation using foam height reduce measurement error? | Measure from the same reference point with a ruler and take readings at the same stated time; an uneven or collapsing foam surface is a source of uncertainty. |
| 20 | How can tissue-diffusion investigations reduce variation between samples? | Cut samples to the same dimensions and use the same tissue type; keep solution volume, concentration, and exposure time constant. |
| 21 | How can the effect of exercise on breathing or pulse be measured more consistently? | Standardize the exercise, its duration/speed, the recovery interval, and the point at which breathing/pulse is counted; use comparable participants or record relevant differences. |
| 22 | What should be controlled when comparing growth or germination? | Keep other conditions constant, such as temperature, light, water, and growing medium; change only the independent variable being tested. |
| 23 | How can a changing hot-water temperature be prevented from affecting results? | Use a thermostatically controlled bath, insulate/cover the container, or monitor and restore the temperature; apply the same conditions to all samples. |
| 24 | How can readings from a scale be made less subjective? | Read at eye level/perpendicular to the scale and use apparatus with suitable graduations; use a colorimeter or other instrument for colour endpoints. |
| 25 | How can a method be improved if one run or one participant gives an unusual result? | Repeat the measurements/whole investigation, compare results, and calculate a mean from consistent results. |

## Chemistry

| # | Typical question | Markscheme answer / points to include |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | How can a fixed volume of a solution be measured more accurately? | Use a volumetric pipette instead of a measuring cylinder. |
| 2 | How can a variable volume, such as titrant, be measured more accurately? | Use a burette; read the scale at eye level and record the initial and final readings. |
| 3 | Why does replacing a glass beaker with a polystyrene cup improve calorimetry results? | Polystyrene is a better insulator, so less heat is lost to the surroundings. |
| 4 | How can heat loss from a reaction vessel be reduced? | Insulate it with cotton wool/lagging or use a lid; a polystyrene cup is also accepted. |
| 5 | Why does wrapping a heated tube or beaker in cotton wool improve accuracy? | It reduces heat loss, so the measured temperature change is closer to the reaction’s temperature change. |
| 6 | How can the temperature be maintained during a reaction? | Use a water bath at the required temperature and check/control it with a thermometer. |
| 7 | Why should reactants be brought to the required temperature before mixing? | Otherwise the reaction starts while the mixture is still heating, so the starting temperature is wrong. |
| 8 | How can a cooling or temperature-change investigation produce a clearer result? | Take readings at shorter, regular time intervals; plot the points and use the trend/graph consistently. |
| 9 | What should be done to improve the reliability of temperature or titration results? | Repeat the experiment and calculate a mean; repeat anomalous results. |
| 10 | How can volumes be measured more accurately in a heat-change experiment? | Measure with a pipette or burette rather than a measuring cylinder. The scheme commonly accepts this because the graduations are more precise. |
| 11 | How can water left in a boiling tube affect results, and how is this avoided? | It dilutes or changes the next sample; dry/clean the tube or use a fresh tube. |
| 12 | How can the mass of a solid reactant be measured more accurately? | Use a balance (ideally with suitable resolution) rather than estimating the amount. |
| 13 | How can gas volume in a reaction be measured more accurately? | Use a gas syringe, check that the apparatus is airtight, and read the syringe scale carefully. |
| 14 | How can gas collection error from gas escaping at the start be reduced? | Fit/check the apparatus before starting and begin timing consistently as soon as the reactants are mixed. |
| 15 | How can reaction rates be compared fairly? | Keep the starting quantities, concentrations, volumes, temperature, and exposed surface area constant; change only the intended variable. |
| 16 | How can a reaction with a visible endpoint be measured more accurately? | Add reagent dropwise near the endpoint, swirl consistently, and use a white background or suitable indicator to see the first permanent change. |
| 17 | Why might a burette not improve the result in every experiment? | If reaction begins while the solution is still being added (or addition is too slow), the conditions change during addition; follow the specified mixing/timing method. |
| 18 | How can a temperature maximum or minimum be recorded more accurately? | Use a thermometer/probe with appropriate graduations and take frequent readings, especially near the turning point. |
| 19 | How can heat loss during transfer be reduced? | Use an insulated vessel and transfer quickly; measure initial/final temperatures promptly and consistently. |
| 20 | How can uncertainty from the starting temperature be reduced? | Allow all reactants to reach the specified temperature first and measure it immediately before mixing. |
| 21 | How can inconsistent mixing be reduced as a source of error? | Stir each mixture in the same way and for the same duration; start timing at the same point in the procedure. |
| 22 | How can volume readings avoid parallax error? | Read the meniscus at eye level, with the scale viewed perpendicularly. |
| 23 | How can a precipitate-height measurement be improved? | Use a ruler with fine divisions, keep the vessel upright on a level surface, and read the height at eye level using the same endpoint each time. |
| 24 | How can contamination between reagents be prevented? | Use clean, dry apparatus and separate droppers/pipettes, or rinse thoroughly between solutions. |
| 25 | What is a good general improvement when the method uses estimated quantities or only one trial? | Replace estimates with suitable volumetric/balance measurements and repeat the experiment to calculate a mean. State which error each change reduces. |

## Physics

| # | Typical question | Markscheme answer / points to include |
|---:|---|---|
| 1 | Why is timing many oscillations more accurate than timing one? | Start/end reaction-time errors are a smaller fraction of the total time; divide the total by the number of oscillations. |
| 2 | How can the period of a pendulum be measured more accurately? | Time 10 or 20 oscillations, then divide by the number timed. |
| 3 | How can timing of oscillations be made more consistent? | Use a fiducial marker and start/stop as the bob passes the same reference point, usually the vertical. |
| 4 | How can distance to or from a pendulum bob be measured accurately? | Keep the rule close to the bob and use a set square or similar aid to transfer the position to the scale. |
| 5 | How can the dimensions of an irregular block be measured more accurately? | Measure each dimension at several positions and take the mean. |
| 6 | What is a common source of inaccuracy when measuring an irregular object? | Its dimensions are difficult to define/read precisely; repeat measurements in several places and average them. |
| 7 | How can volume displacement for a small object be made more accurate? | Use a measuring cylinder with smaller divisions; use several objects together to produce a larger measurable change, then divide by their number. |
| 8 | How can a sphere’s volume be found more accurately? | Measure its diameter/radius at several orientations and calculate the volume; alternatively use finer cylinder graduations, several spheres, and a mean. |
| 9 | How can a small liquid-volume reading be improved? | Use a narrower measuring cylinder or one with smaller scale divisions; read the meniscus at eye level. |
| 10 | How are line-of-sight/parallax errors avoided? | Read the scale at eye level and at right angles to it. |
| 11 | Why can a larger measured value be more accurate in percentage terms? | A similar absolute reading error is a smaller percentage of a larger value. |
| 12 | How can repeated length, time, or voltage readings be used? | Repeat each reading and calculate the mean; check any anomalous result. |
| 13 | How can a graph-based result be made more accurate? | Take more readings over a wider/suitable range, plot carefully, and draw a best-fit line. |
| 14 | Why can a graph give a more accurate value than one reading? | The gradient/line uses many measurements and averages random scatter; anomalies are easier to spot. |
| 15 | How can a focal-length experiment using an image be improved? | Use a fixed/translucent screen or a grid on the screen so the image height can be measured without blocking the light. |
| 16 | What makes ray-tracing measurements inaccurate, and what helps? | A thick ray or uncertain ray centre/alignment affects positions; use thin rays, mark the centre carefully, and align pins/crosses consistently. |
| 17 | How can a spring-extension reading be improved? | Read the scale at eye level, wait for oscillations to stop, and measure extension from the unloaded length using a fixed pointer. |
| 18 | How can spring readings improve the estimate of a gradient or spring constant? | Use several loads/extensions across a suitable range and find the gradient of a best-fit graph. |
| 19 | What are valid sources of uncertainty in a carefully performed measurement? | Examples include imperfect ruler graduations, a load not exactly its stated value, non-linear spring extension, or difficulty locating an endpoint. Name one relevant to the setup. |
| 20 | How can measuring the diameter of a thin rod be improved? | Wrap string around several turns and measure the total circumference/length, then calculate the diameter; or use a more suitable gauge/measure at several points and average. |
| 21 | How can an electrical experiment’s value be improved using a graph? | Take more readings at different potential differences and use the graph/gradient to average the results; choose a useful scale. |
| 22 | How can heat-loss measurements be made more consistent? | Measure equal volumes at the same starting temperature, use the same container/thermometer position, and take readings at regular intervals. |
| 23 | How can a measured depth or height be transferred more accurately? | Mark the level while the object/apparatus is in position, remove it if needed, and measure the marked distance with a rule. |
| 24 | How can an optical image height be read without disturbing the image? | Use a translucent screen viewed from behind or attach a grid/scale to the screen; keep the screen fixed. |
| 25 | How can a trolley’s average speed between two points be measured more accurately? | Measure the distance between fixed marks, time the trolley over that same distance, repeat, and average; use light gates if available to reduce reaction-time error. |

## A useful exam-writing pattern

For a source-of-error question, make the answer a matched pair: **specific source of error → specific improvement**. If asked for an explanation, say how the error changes the reading/result. For accuracy questions, choose a measurement method with finer divisions or a larger measurable signal; for reliability questions, repeat and average. Keep control variables constant when the question is testing a fair comparison.
